# quartzite Comes Back

*Theme: Incident response*

The first report was a paste in the helpdesk channel, from a regular who sat in
three of the affected channels and had thought to copy the text before it
scrolled. `quartzite` was putting the same shortened URL into every channel where
it held ops, working down the list at a channel every eight seconds or so, and it
had opped two nicks nobody could place.

Ola Bergqvist had the services pager that week. The account was a founder on
eleven channels. She suspended it before she had any theory at all about how it
had been taken.

```
/msg NickServ SUSPEND quartzite +30d compromised, pending contact with owner
-NickServ- Nick quartzite is now suspended.
```

`ns_suspend` hangs an `NSSuspendInfo` off the account. Then it walks the group.
Anyone online under any alias gets `Logout()` and is handed to NickServ's
collision handling, so `quartzite` was a guest nick inside two seconds and the
ops went with it. Each alias also has its `last_quit` set to the reason. Ola had
typed that reason for the next oper to read; `NickServ INFO` prints it as *Last
quit message* to anybody who asks, the account having never set `HIDE_QUIT`. She
found that out a day later from a user who had gone looking.

Every way back in that she could think of consults the flag. IDENTIFY answers
`Nick %s is currently suspended.` and stops. So do RECOVER, CERT, ACCESS, AJOIN
and GROUP, each carrying its own copy of the test.

She deopped the two unknown nicks and put a timestamp in the staff channel.
Someone asked whether this was the same thing as the week before. It wasn't, and
explaining why took a while.

Then the owner. `NickServ INFO quartzite ALL` gives an oper the `Email address`
field whether or not the owner has set `HIDE_EMAIL`, so she took it from there.
*Your account on the network was used to post spam this evening. It is suspended
for thirty days. If this was not you, reply to this and we will work out how they
got in.*

She copied the log off to her home directory — `services.log.20260922`, the date
appended by the default log block, which catches everything that is not an
OperServ command — and did not open it. The owner took the next forty minutes.

At 21:49 the helpdesk channel went again. `quartzite` was in `#photo-crit`. With
ops.

Her first guess was a client certificate. Add a fingerprint to the cert list
earlier in the evening and the IRCd hands it over on the next connection;
services identify off that, with no password typed at all. `CERT LIST` still
answers on a suspended account, because `ns_cert` dispatches LIST before it gets
near the suspension check. Three entries. Two matched the owner's usual host. The
third was a /64 she could not place, and she gave it most of half an hour — a
whois, then the RIR, then a mail to the one colleague who might still be awake —
before it turned out the next morning to be the owner's phone on a carrier range.
She would have had to look it up either way.

The guess was wrong regardless. `NSCert::OnFingerprint` looks the account up, and
the line after the lookup is `if (!nc || nc->HasExt("NS_SUSPENDED")) return;`.
She had read straight past it twice.

So she opened the log. Two lines, ninety seconds apart:

```
[Sep 22 21:47:39 2026] COMMAND: Guest48119!~q@2a01:...:4f21 used RESETPASS for quartzite (group: quartzite)
[Sep 22 21:49:11 2026] COMMAND: Guest48119!~q@2a01:...:4f21 used CONFIRM to confirm RESETPASS and forcefully identify as quartzite
```

Ola had not thought about RESETPASS in years. She went and read the module.

The command itself is unremarkable. It is declared `AllowUnregistered(true)`, so
any connection may type it, identified or not, from anywhere. That is what the
command is for. It takes a nickname and an email address and compares the address
against the one on the account with `equals_ci`. On a match it calls
`SendResetEmail`, which generates `Anope::Random(20)` and stashes it on the
account as a `ResetInfo` with the current time. Then it mails the code out. The
body is `mail:reset_message` from the config, and it spells the next move out for
whoever opens the mail: *To reset your password, type " /msg NickServ CONFIRM %n
%c "*.

Ola spent longer on the other half of the module. `NSResetPass::OnPreCommand` is
a hook rather than a command. It watches everything that goes past for
`nickserv/confirm` with two parameters. Where it finds a stored `ResetInfo` under
3600 seconds old, and the second parameter matches the code under `equals_cs`, it
unsets the reset, shrinks the account's `UNCONFIRMED` flag, and calls:

```cpp
source.GetUser()->Identify(na);
```

```
-NickServ- You are now identified for your nick. Change your password now.
```

No password is set anywhere along that path. Nothing about the account changes
except that a connection is now logged into it. The suspension checks Ola had
leaned on at ten past nine all sit inside IDENTIFY, and this does not go through
IDENTIFY; in the version they were running, nothing on the path asked about the
flag. That is the 21:49 line. `ns_resetpass` writes it and attributes it to
CONFIRM, because the logger prints the command the user typed.

That left 21:47. All RESETPASS checks before it generates a passcode is
`equals_ci` against `na->nc->email`, and the passcode goes to the address on the
account. For 21:47 to be followed ninety seconds later by 21:49, the connection
on that IPv6 address was reading the owner's mail. Ola's own message had gone
into that mailbox at ten past nine.

She sat with that one for a while.

Anope still ships the thing RESETPASS replaced. `ns_getpass` takes a nickname
from an oper and runs `Anope::Decrypt(na->nc->pass, tmp_pass)`; on a return of 1
it replies `Password for %s is \002%s\002.` The help text warns that use of it is
logged and sent out as a WALLOPS/GLOBOPS, and the example config duly lists
`nickserv/getpass` in the globops log block, in among the akills and the jupes.
That was the recovery procedure. You told an oper you had lost your password and
an oper read it back to you.

Their install had come up on `enc_sha256`, the shipped config's default;
`enc_md5`, `enc_sha1` and `enc_old` are marked deprecated there, kept for
upgrading away from. Under sha256 `Anope::Decrypt` has nothing to hand back, and
GETPASS answers `GETPASS command unavailable because encryption is in use.` The
password is not recoverable because it is not stored. So RESETPASS moves the
proof of identity onto the one thing services can still compare against the
database, which is the address you registered with. And it has to be usable by
someone who cannot log in, since that is the situation it addresses. `mail:delay`
is the brake on using it to mail-bomb a stranger. Five minutes, by default.

She read the fix over breakfast, after running `apt source anope` on her laptop.
Their services were built from the upstream 2.0.12 tarball rather than the
distribution package; they had wanted a module out of `extras/` three years ago
and nobody ever went back. In `debian/patches` sat `CVE-2024-30187.patch`, dated
17 February 2024, from Sadie Powell, subject *Prevent resetting the password of
suspended accounts*, closing upstream issue 351. Eight added lines.

One `HasExt("NS_SUSPENDED")` test goes in `Execute`, above the email comparison,
so RESETPASS on a suspended account replies `Nick %s is currently suspended.` and
generates no passcode at all. At 21:47 the account was already suspended, so that
test on its own would have covered her evening. The second goes at the top of the
`OnPreCommand` block and returns `EVENT_STOP` before the passcode is compared.
That one is for the ordering she happened not to get: a passcode mailed before
the suspend and confirmed after it, still inside its hour.

The owner wrote back on Thursday, from a different address. Same password on the
mailbox as on a forum that had been breached in the spring. No idea when the
mailbox had gone.

Ola put the patch into the build tree and rebuilt it that morning. The restart
waited a week for the Tuesday window. There was nothing much to do in between:
the account was suspended, and whoever had the mailbox had stopped.

The runbook entry under *compromised account* picked up two lines under the
SUSPEND command. Grep `services.log` for `used RESETPASS for` on the account.
And where the mailbox itself is in question, move the account's address to one
the owner has confirmed out of band, with `NickServ SASET <nick> EMAIL
<address>`, which an oper can set outright — `nickserv:forceemail` defaults to
yes, so the field cannot be emptied, and wherever it points is where the reset
goes.

After the restart she typed the command at it herself and pasted the answer into
the ticket.

```
/msg NickServ RESETPASS quartzite <address>
-NickServ- Nick quartzite is currently suspended.
```

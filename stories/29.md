# quartzite Comes Back

*Theme: Incident response*

The report came in through the helpdesk channel at 21:08, from a regular who had
been in three of the affected channels and had the sense to copy the text before
it scrolled: `quartzite` was posting the same shortened URL into every channel
where they held ops, roughly one channel every eight seconds, and had opped two
nicks nobody recognised along the way.

Ola Bergqvist had the services pager that week. The account was a founder on
eleven channels, so she suspended it before she had any idea how it had been
taken.

```
/msg NickServ SUSPEND quartzite +30d compromised, pending contact with owner
-NickServ- Nick quartzite is now suspended.
```

`ns_suspend` hangs an `NSSuspendInfo` off the account, recording who suspended
it, when, the reason and when it expires. Then it walks every alias in the
group, calls `Logout()` on anyone online under one of them, and hands the nick
to NickServ's collision handling. Two seconds later `quartzite` was a guest nick
with no ops anywhere. Anything that tried to authenticate to the account
afterwards hit the suspension check: `ns_identify` answers `Nick %s is currently
suspended.` and stops, and so do RECOVER, CERT, ACCESS, AJOIN and GROUP, each
with its own copy of the same test.

Ola deopped the two unknown nicks and put a note in the staff channel with the
timestamp. The default log block on their install catches
`commands = "~operserv/* *"`, so everything that is not an OperServ command
lands in `services.log` with the date appended to the filename. She copied
`services.log.20260922` out to her home directory, which she did not open for
another forty minutes, because the first thing she did was write to the owner.
`NickServ INFO quartzite ALL` prints the `Email address` field to an oper
whether or not the owner has set `HIDE_EMAIL`; she took it from there. *Your
account on the network was used to post spam this evening. It is suspended for
thirty days. If this was not you, reply to this and we will work out how they
got in.*

At 21:49 the helpdesk channel lit up again. `quartzite` was in `#photo-crit`
with ops.

Her first thought was a client certificate. If whoever had the account had added
a fingerprint to the cert list at some point during the evening, the IRCd would
hand it to services on the next connection and services would identify them
without a password ever being typed. She ran `CERT LIST` against the account,
which still works while an account is suspended because `ns_cert` dispatches
LIST before it reaches the suspension check, and got three entries. Two she
could match to the owner's usual host. The third took her until about ten past
ten to attribute, at which point it turned out to be the owner's phone.

It would not have worked in any case. `NSCert::OnFingerprint` looks up the
account, and the line after the lookup is
`if (!nc || nc->HasExt("NS_SUSPENDED")) return;`. She had read past it twice
while looking for something else.

The log had it in two lines, ninety seconds apart:

```
[Sep 22 21:47:39 2026] COMMAND: Guest48119!~q@2a01:...:4f21 used RESETPASS for quartzite (group: quartzite)
[Sep 22 21:49:11 2026] COMMAND: Guest48119!~q@2a01:...:4f21 used CONFIRM to confirm RESETPASS and forcefully identify as quartzite
```

Ola had not thought about RESETPASS in years. She went and read the module.

`ns_resetpass` provides one command and one hook. The command is declared with
`AllowUnregistered(true)`, meaning any connection may type it, identified or
otherwise, from anywhere. It takes a nickname and an email address; it compares
the address against the one on the account with `equals_ci`; and if they match
it calls `SendResetEmail`, which generates `Anope::Random(20)`, stashes it on
the account as a `ResetInfo` with the current time, and mails it to the address
on the account. The mail body is `mail:reset_message` out of the config, and it
spells the next step out for whoever opens it: *To reset your password, type
" /msg NickServ CONFIRM %n %c "*.

The hook is the half that matters. `NSResetPass::OnPreCommand` watches for
`nickserv/confirm` with two parameters, looks up the stored `ResetInfo`, checks
that it is less than 3600 seconds old, compares the passcode with `equals_cs`,
and on a match clears the account's `UNCONFIRMED` flag and calls
`source.GetUser()->Identify(na)`. No new password is set anywhere along it. That
is the 21:49 line in the log, written by `ns_resetpass` but attributed to
CONFIRM, because the logger prints the command the user actually typed.

```
-NickServ- You are now identified for your nick. Change your password now.
```

The suspension checks Ola had relied on at 21:12 all live inside IDENTIFY. This
path runs in a pre-command hook on CONFIRM and calls `Identify()` directly. In
the version they were running, nothing along it consulted the suspension, and a
module filed under passwords had been logging people in through a hook on
somebody else's command for as long as it had shipped.

The other half of it took her longer to say out loud. `equals_ci` against
`na->nc->email` is all RESETPASS checks before it generates a passcode, and the
passcode goes to the address on the account. For the 21:47 line to be followed
ninety seconds later by the 21:49 line, the connection on that IPv6 address had
to be reading the owner's mail. That mailbox had already had one message from
her that evening, sent at 21:12 to the address out of `INFO`.

RESETPASS exists because of what services cannot do once passwords are hashed,
and the shape of that gap is visible in a command Anope still ships next to it.
`ns_getpass` provides GETPASS: an oper types a nickname, Anope calls
`Anope::Decrypt(na->nc->pass, tmp_pass)`, and if that returns 1 the oper is shown
the password in plain text. Its own help text warns that the use is logged and
sent out as a WALLOPS/GLOBOPS, and the example config duly lists
`nickserv/getpass` in the globops log block, in among the akills and the jupes.
That was the recovery procedure: a human with database access reads your secret
back to you, and the network watches it happen.

`enc_md5`, `enc_sha1` and `enc_old` are marked deprecated in the example config,
kept for upgrading away from and slated for removal. Their install had come up
on `enc_sha256`, the module the shipped config enables. `Anope::Decrypt` returns
nothing useful under it, and GETPASS answers
`GETPASS command unavailable because encryption is in use.` The password
genuinely is not there to be recovered. So RESETPASS moves the proof of identity
to something services can still check, the address you registered with. It is
deliberately usable by someone who cannot log in, since that is the situation it
addresses, and `mail:delay`, five minutes by default, is there to stop it being
a way to mail-bomb a stranger.

She read the fix over breakfast the next day, after pulling `apt source anope`
on her laptop, because their services were built from the upstream 2.0.12
tarball rather than the distribution package. They had wanted a module out of
`extras/` three years ago and never went back. In the Ubuntu package's
`debian/patches` series sat `CVE-2024-30187.patch`, dated 17 February 2024, from
Sadie Powell, subject *Prevent resetting the password of suspended accounts*,
closing upstream issue 351. Eight added lines. One `HasExt("NS_SUSPENDED")` test
in `Execute`, so RESETPASS on a suspended account replies `Nick %s is currently
suspended.` and never generates a passcode. One more at the top of the
`OnPreCommand` block, returning `EVENT_STOP` before the passcode is compared.

The first of the two covers her evening: at 21:47 the account was already
suspended, so RESETPASS would have answered and stopped without mailing
anything. The second covers the ordering she did not get, a passcode mailed
before the suspend and confirmed after, still inside its hour.

The owner replied on Thursday, from a different address, having used the same
password on the mailbox as on a forum breached in the spring. They could not say
when the mailbox had gone.

Ola applied the patch to the build tree, rebuilt, and restarted services during
the Tuesday window. The runbook entry under *compromised account* grew two lines
below the SUSPEND command: check `services.log` for `used RESETPASS for` on the
account, and if the mailbox itself is suspect, move the account's address to one
the owner has confirmed out of band with `NickServ SASET <nick> EMAIL <address>`,
which an oper may set outright. `nickserv:forceemail` defaults to yes, so there
is no unsetting it; the reset path points wherever that field points.

Then she opened a second connection, typed the command herself, and pasted the
answer into the ticket.

```
/msg NickServ RESETPASS quartzite <address>
-NickServ- Nick quartzite is currently suspended.
```

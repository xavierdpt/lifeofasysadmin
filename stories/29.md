# Forty Minutes After the Suspend

*Theme: Incident response*

The report came in through the helpdesk channel at 21:08, from a regular who had
been in three of the affected channels and had the sense to copy the text before
it scrolled: `quartzite` was posting the same shortened URL into every channel
where they held ops, roughly one channel every eight seconds, and had opped two
nicks nobody recognised along the way.

Ola Bergqvist had the services pager that week. She did the containment first and
the thinking afterwards, in that order, because the account was a founder on
eleven channels and the thinking could take as long as it liked once the account
stopped being able to do anything.

```
/msg NickServ SUSPEND quartzite +30d compromised, pending contact with owner
-NickServ- Nick quartzite is now suspended.
```

That is a fairly violent command under the hood. `ns_suspend` hangs an
`NSSuspendInfo` off the account — who suspended it, when, the reason, when it
expires — and then walks every alias in the group, calls `Logout()` on anyone
online under one of them, and hands the nick to NickServ's collision handling.
Two seconds later `quartzite` was a guest nick with no ops anywhere. Anything
that tried to authenticate to the account afterwards hit the suspension check:
`ns_identify` answers `Nick %s is currently suspended.` and stops, and so do
RECOVER, CERT, ACCESS, AJOIN and GROUP, each with its own copy of the same test.

Ola deopped the two unknown nicks, put a note in the staff channel with the
timestamp, and started on evidence. The default log block on their install
catches `commands = "~operserv/* *"`, so everything that is not an OperServ
command lands in `services.log` with the date appended to the filename. She
copied `services.log.20260922` out to her home directory before anything could
rotate it, which is a habit she has had since a colleague once truncated the only
copy of an access log with a careless redirect.

Then she wrote to the address on the account, which is the part of the job that
takes actual time. *Your account on the network was used to post spam this
evening. It is suspended for thirty days. If this was not you, reply to this and
we will work out how they got in.*

At 21:49 the helpdesk channel lit up again. `quartzite` was in `#photo-crit` with
ops.

---

Her first assumption was that she had fumbled the suspend — typed an alias that
had since been ungrouped, or hit a different account whose display name looked
similar. `NickServ INFO` said otherwise: suspended, by her, at 21:12, reason
intact. And `ChanServ` was handing out the ops entirely correctly, because
ChanServ access hangs off the *account*, and something had told services that the
connection in `#photo-crit` was logged into that account.

The log had it in two lines, forty minutes apart from the suspend and about
ninety seconds apart from each other:

```
[Sep 22 21:47:39 2026] COMMAND: Guest48119!~q@2a01:...:4f21 used RESETPASS for quartzite (group: quartzite)
[Sep 22 21:49:11 2026] COMMAND: Guest48119!~q@2a01:...:4f21 used CONFIRM to confirm RESETPASS and forcefully identify as quartzite
```

The second line is written by `ns_resetpass` but attributed to CONFIRM, because
the log records the command the user actually typed.

Ola had not thought about RESETPASS in years, which is roughly the point at which
you stop understanding what it does.

`ns_resetpass` provides one command and one hook. The command is declared with
`AllowUnregistered(true)`, meaning any connection may type it, identified or
otherwise, from anywhere. It takes a nickname and an email address; it compares
the address against the one on the account with `equals_ci`; and if they match it
calls `SendResetEmail`, which generates `Anope::Random(20)`, stashes it on the
account as a `ResetInfo` with the current time, and mails it out using
`mail:reset_subject` and `mail:reset_message`.

The hook is the half that matters. `NSResetPass::OnPreCommand` watches for
`nickserv/confirm` with two parameters, looks up the stored `ResetInfo`, checks
that it is less than 3600 seconds old, compares the passcode with `equals_cs` —
and on a match clears the account's `UNCONFIRMED` flag and calls
`source.GetUser()->Identify(na)`.

It does not set a new password. It logs you in, and says so:

```
-NickServ- You are now identified for your nick. Change your password now.
```

Everything Ola had relied on at 21:12 was a check inside IDENTIFY. This path
never reaches IDENTIFY. It runs in a pre-command hook on a completely different
command, in a module whose name suggests it deals with passwords, and it calls
`Identify()` directly. In the version they were running, the only thing between a
suspended account and a live session on it was possession of the registered
mailbox — and the person spamming those channels had the mailbox, because the
same reused password had opened both.

---

RESETPASS exists because of what services *cannot* do once passwords are hashed,
and the shape of that gap is visible in a command Anope still ships next to it.
`ns_getpass` provides `GETPASS`: an oper types a nickname, Anope calls
`Anope::Decrypt(na->nc->pass, tmp_pass)`, and if that returns 1 the oper is shown
the password in plain text. Its own help text warns that the use is logged and
sent out as a WALLOPS/GLOBOPS, and the example config duly lists
`nickserv/getpass` in the globops log block, alongside the akills and the jupes.
That was the recovery procedure: a human with access to the database reads your
secret back to you, and the whole network watches it happen.

With `enc_bcrypt` or `enc_sha256` loaded — and `enc_md5` and `enc_sha1` were
marked deprecated in 2.0.6 for the obvious reasons — `Anope::Decrypt` returns
nothing useful, and GETPASS answers `GETPASS command unavailable because
encryption is in use.` The password genuinely is not there to be recovered. So
RESETPASS moves the proof of identity somewhere services can actually check it:
the address you registered with. It is deliberately usable by someone who cannot
log in, since that is the entire situation it addresses, and `mail:delay`, five
minutes by default, is what stops it being a way to mail-bomb a stranger.

Which makes it an authentication path. Ola's containment had covered the door
marked *authentication* and left this one open, and so had upstream, for about as
long as the command had existed.

She read the fix over breakfast the next day, after pulling `apt source anope` on
her laptop, because their services were built from the upstream 2.0.12 tarball
rather than the distribution package — they had wanted a module out of `extras/`
three years ago and never went back. In the Ubuntu package's `debian/patches`
series sat `CVE-2024-30187.patch`, dated February 2024, subject *Prevent resetting
the password of suspended accounts*, closing upstream issue 351. Eight added
lines. One `HasExt("NS_SUSPENDED")` test in `Execute`, so RESETPASS on a
suspended account replies `Nick %s is currently suspended.` and never generates a
passcode. One more in `OnPreCommand`, returning `EVENT_STOP` before the passcode
is compared.

The second check is the one Ola stared at longest. It is there for the ordering
she did not get: a passcode mailed *before* the suspend and confirmed after, still
inside its hour, walking through a door that was locked in the meantime. Her
attacker had simply asked for a fresh one.

She applied the patch to the build tree, rebuilt, and restarted services during
the Tuesday window. Then she opened the suspend back up in her notes and added a
line to the staff runbook under *compromised account*, below the SUSPEND command:
check `services.log` for `used RESETPASS for` on the account, and if the mailbox
itself is suspect, move the account's address to one the owner has confirmed out
of band — `NickServ SASET <nick> EMAIL <address>`, which an oper may set outright
— before you walk away from it. `nickserv:forceemail` defaults to yes, so there
is no unsetting it; the reset path points wherever that field points.

The owner of `quartzite` replied on Thursday. They had been meaning to change that
password since the forum breach in spring.

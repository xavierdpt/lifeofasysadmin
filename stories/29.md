# quartzite Comes Back

*Theme: Incident response*

The first report was a paste in the helpdesk channel, from a regular who sat in
three of the affected channels and thought to copy the text before it scrolled.
`quartzite` was putting the same shortened URL into every channel where it held
ops, working down the list at a channel every eight seconds or so, and it had
opped two nicks nobody could place.

Ola Bergqvist had the services pager that week. The account was a founder on
eleven channels. She suspended it before she had any theory at all about how it
had been taken.

```
/msg NickServ SUSPEND quartzite +30d compromised, pending contact with owner
-NickServ- Nick quartzite is now suspended.
```

`ns_suspend` hangs an `NSSuspendInfo` off the account, then walks the group,
calls `Logout()` on anyone online under any alias in it and hands each nick to
NickServ's collision handling. `quartzite` became a guest nick inside two seconds
and the ops went with it. Every way back in that Ola could think of consults the
flag: IDENTIFY answers `Nick %s is currently suspended.` and stops, and so do
RECOVER, CERT, ACCESS, AJOIN and GROUP, each carrying its own copy of the test.

She deopped the two unknown nicks, dropped a timestamp into the staff channel,
and went to write to the owner. `NickServ INFO quartzite ALL` gives an oper the
`Email address` field whether or not the owner has set `HIDE_EMAIL`, so she took
it from there. *Your account on the network was used to post spam this evening.
It is suspended for thirty days. If this was not you, reply to this and we will
work out how they got in.*

She copied the log off to her home directory — `services.log.20260922`, the date
appended by the default log block, which catches everything that is not an
OperServ command — and did not open it. The owner took the next forty minutes.

At 21:49 the helpdesk channel went again. `quartzite` was in `#photo-crit`. With
ops.

Her first guess was a client certificate. If whoever had the account had added a
fingerprint to the cert list earlier in the evening, the IRCd would hand it over
on the next connection and services would identify them without a password being
typed at all. `CERT LIST` still answers on a suspended account, because `ns_cert`
dispatches LIST before it gets near the suspension check, and it came back with
three entries. Two matched the owner's usual host. The third she did not manage
to place that night; it turned out the next morning to be the owner's phone, on a
carrier range she would have had to look up either way. It was the wrong guess
anyway: `NSCert::OnFingerprint` looks the account up, and the line after the
lookup is `if (!nc || nc->HasExt("NS_SUSPENDED")) return;`. She had read straight
past it twice.

Then the log. Two lines, ninety seconds apart:

```
[Sep 22 21:47:39 2026] COMMAND: Guest48119!~q@2a01:...:4f21 used RESETPASS for quartzite (group: quartzite)
[Sep 22 21:49:11 2026] COMMAND: Guest48119!~q@2a01:...:4f21 used CONFIRM to confirm RESETPASS and forcefully identify as quartzite
```

Ola had not thought about RESETPASS in years. She went and read the module.

The command itself is unremarkable. It is declared `AllowUnregistered(true)`, so
any connection may type it, identified or not, from anywhere, which is what the
command is for. It takes a nickname and an email address, compares the address
against the one on the account with `equals_ci`, and on a match calls
`SendResetEmail`. That generates `Anope::Random(20)`, stashes it on the account
as a `ResetInfo` with the current time, and mails it out. The body is
`mail:reset_message` from the config, which spells the next move out for whoever
opens the mail: *To reset your password, type " /msg NickServ CONFIRM %n %c "*.

Ola spent longer on the other half of the module. `NSResetPass::OnPreCommand` is
a hook rather than a command. It watches everything that goes past for
`nickserv/confirm` with two parameters, finds the stored `ResetInfo`, checks it
is under 3600 seconds old, and compares the second parameter with `equals_cs`. On
a match it unsets the reset, shrinks the account's `UNCONFIRMED` flag, and calls:

```cpp
source.GetUser()->Identify(na);
```

```
-NickServ- You are now identified for your nick. Change your password now.
```

No password is set anywhere along that path. Nothing about the account changes
except that a connection is now logged into it. The suspension checks Ola had
leaned on at ten past nine all sit inside IDENTIFY, and this does not go through
IDENTIFY; in the version they were running, nothing on the path asked about the
flag. That is the 21:49 line, written by `ns_resetpass` and attributed to CONFIRM
because the logger prints the command the user typed.

Which left 21:47. All RESETPASS checks before it generates a passcode is
`equals_ci` against `na->nc->email`, and the passcode goes to the address on the
account. For 21:47 to be followed ninety seconds later by 21:49, the connection
on that IPv6 address was reading the owner's mail. Ola's own message had gone
into that mailbox at ten past nine.

Anope still ships the thing RESETPASS replaced. `ns_getpass` takes a nickname
from an oper, runs `Anope::Decrypt(na->nc->pass, tmp_pass)`, and on a return of 1
replies `Password for %s is \002%s\002.` The help text warns that use of it is
logged and sent out as a WALLOPS/GLOBOPS, and the example config duly lists
`nickserv/getpass` in the globops log block, in among the akills and the jupes.
That was the recovery procedure: you told an oper you had lost your password, and
an oper read it back to you.

Their install had come up on `enc_sha256`, which is what the shipped config
enables; `enc_md5`, `enc_sha1` and `enc_old` are marked deprecated there, kept
for upgrading away from. Under sha256 `Anope::Decrypt` has nothing to hand back,
and GETPASS answers `GETPASS command unavailable because encryption is in use.`
The password is not recoverable because it is not stored. So RESETPASS moves the
proof of identity onto the one thing services can still compare against the
database, which is the address you registered with, and it has to be usable by
someone who cannot log in, since that is the situation it addresses.
`mail:delay`, five minutes by default, is the brake on using it to mail-bomb a
stranger.

She read the fix over breakfast, after running `apt source anope` on her laptop.
Their services were built from the upstream 2.0.12 tarball rather than the
distribution package; they had wanted a module out of `extras/` three years ago
and nobody ever went back. In `debian/patches` sat `CVE-2024-30187.patch`, dated
17 February 2024, from Sadie Powell, subject *Prevent resetting the password of
suspended accounts*, closing upstream issue 351. Eight added lines. One
`HasExt("NS_SUSPENDED")` test in `Execute`, above the email comparison, so
RESETPASS on a suspended account replies `Nick %s is currently suspended.` and
generates no passcode at all; at 21:47 the account was already suspended, so that
test alone covers her evening. The second goes at the top of the `OnPreCommand`
block and returns `EVENT_STOP` before the passcode is compared, for the ordering
she happened not to get, a passcode mailed before the suspend and confirmed after
it, still inside its hour.

The owner wrote back on Thursday from a different address. Same password on the
mailbox as on a forum that had been breached in the spring. No idea when the
mailbox had gone.

Ola put the patch into the build tree, rebuilt, and restarted services in the
Tuesday window. The runbook entry under *compromised account* picked up two lines
under the SUSPEND command: grep `services.log` for `used RESETPASS for` on the
account, and, where the mailbox itself is in question, move the account's address
to one the owner has confirmed out of band with `NickServ SASET <nick> EMAIL
<address>`, which an oper can set outright. `nickserv:forceemail` defaults to
yes, so there is no unsetting the field, and the reset path points wherever it
points.

Then she opened a second connection, typed the command herself, and pasted the
answer into the ticket.

```
/msg NickServ RESETPASS quartzite <address>
-NickServ- Nick quartzite is currently suspended.
```

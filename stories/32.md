# Fourteen Ceiling Units and the Returns Bench

*Theme: On-call*

The page came at 02:21 on a Wednesday. Ines Achterberg was on call for the Kestle Valley
library consortium that week, which meant four branches, a few hundred public PCs and the
Wi-Fi in all of them. The alert said fourteen access points at the Mill Street branch had
stopped checking in with the controller, all within the same two minutes.

Mill Street had a switch firmware upgrade scheduled for 02:00. Ines had approved it on
Monday. The switch in the storeroom powered every ceiling unit in the building over PoE,
so a reboot of the switch was a reboot of every access point, and she had expected a gap
in the graphs. She had not expected the gap to stay open. The switch was back: its uptime
said eighteen minutes, and its MAC table showed an address on each of the fourteen AP
ports. The units had power and a link, and none of them had reached the controller.

The staff Wi-Fi at Mill Street ran on those fourteen units, so there was no remote way
into the building. She drove.

---

The storeroom at Mill Street was also where returns went. The consortium was sending a
dozen old Open Mesh OM2Ps to a school, and the school's IT contractor had asked for them
wiped back to the image they shipped with, not the libraries' build with the consortium's
configuration on it. The bench under the storeroom window was a laptop on the shelf and
six PoE ports on the storeroom switch. Six of the returns had arrived; the other six were
coming from the Harlow Road branch on Wednesday's van. On Tuesday afternoon Lola Fenn
started `ap51-flash` on the laptop, plugged the six units into the bench ports, and
watched them flash side by side. She left them plugged in, and left the program running,
so that when the van came she could swap the next six into the same ports.

The laptop's screen woke to a tmux session. The command at the top of the scrollback was
this:

```
$ sudo ap51-flash eth0 om2p-stock.bin
```

Below it, the six bench units from the afternoon, their lines interleaved as they had
flashed together:

```
[a2:40:1c:06:3e:11]: device type 'OM2P' detected
[a2:40:1c:06:3e:11]: OM2P: tftp client asks for 'fwupgrade.cfg', serving fwupgrade.cfg-OM2P portion of: om2p-stock.bin (1 blocks) ...
[a2:40:1c:06:3e:2f]: device type 'OM2P' detected
[a2:40:1c:06:3e:2f]: OM2P: tftp client asks for 'fwupgrade.cfg', serving fwupgrade.cfg-OM2P portion of: om2p-stock.bin (1 blocks) ...
...
[a2:40:1c:06:3e:11]: OM2P: image successfully transmitted - writing image to flash ...
[a2:40:1c:06:3e:11]: OM2P: flash complete. Device ready to unplug.
```

And below the sixth, with no timestamps to separate them from the afternoon, fourteen
more. Ines copied the MACs out of the scrollback and put them next to the controller's
list of Mill Street APs. They were the ceiling units: the reading room, the children's
floor, the study carrels, the meeting room. `ap51-flash` had written the stock image onto
all of them in the few minutes after the switch came back.

She pressed Ctrl-C, and the program exited.

---

Ines sat on the storeroom floor and opened `ap51-flash(8)` on the laptop.

`ap51-flash` listens on an interface for devices to announce themselves. An Open Mesh
bootloader, when it starts, sends an ARP request for 192.168.100.8, looking for a TFTP
server with new firmware. If nothing answers, the bootloader carries on and boots what is
in flash. The man page describes this as "identification markers" sent "during the initial
boot process of the embedded boot loader". `ap51-flash` answers that ARP from an address
of its own, serves the bootloader a `fwupgrade.cfg` out of the image file, then the files
that config names.

The same section says what the tool spares whoever runs it: it "doesn't require a target
device specific network configuration or TFTP client/server on the same machine". It tells
the model from the ARP request, finds the matching `fwupgrade.cfg-OM2P` inside the
combined image, and prints "Device ready to unplug" when it is done. At the bench, after
one command and one image, the work was plugging units in.

The storeroom port the laptop was plugged into was on the same untagged VLAN as the
ceiling units. A few minutes past two, when the switch finished its upgrade and brought
PoE back, fourteen bootloaders sent the same ARP request Lola's bench units had sent in
the afternoon, and the laptop was still listening. The man page says what happens next, in
the paragraph for `-m`, `--mac`:

> If not specified, all detected devices will be flashed.

The six bench units had rebooted at 02:00 as well, since they were on the same switch. The
scrollback had nothing for them. Ines checked why in the source afterwards: the program
keeps each device it has seen in a list keyed by MAC, and once a device reaches the
"rebooted" state after a flash, any further ARP from it is ignored for the rest of the
run. The six bench units had already been through once. The fourteen ceiling units were
new MACs.

---

The libraries' build server kept the factory image for the OM2P alongside the sysupgrade
one, and Ines copied it onto the bench laptop from her own. She did not want to flash the
six bench units back to the libraries' firmware: they were supposed to leave the building
on the stock image. They were still in the bench ports.

`-m` adds an address to an allowlist, and only the addresses on it are flashed. It has to
be given once per address. The changelog puts it in release 2019.0, "introduced optional
MAC address filtering"; before that, every device that sent a marker the program
recognised was flashed. Ines exported the fourteen MACs from the controller into a file,
one per line, and built the arguments from it:

```
$ args=(); while read -r m; do args+=(-m "$m"); done < millst-aps.txt
$ sudo ap51-flash "${args[@]}" eth0 kvl-om2p-factory.bin
```

Then she went into the switch's web interface and power-cycled PoE on every port at once,
bench ports included, because it was quicker than doing them one by one and because she
wanted to see what the filter did.

The bench units reported first.

```
[a2:40:1c:06:3e:11]: is of type 'OM2P' but MAC does not match MAC filter
[a2:40:1c:06:3e:2f]: is of type 'OM2P' but MAC does not match MAC filter
```

Six of those, and then the ceiling units started coming through, fourteen runs of `device
type 'OM2P' detected` through to `flash complete. Device ready to unplug.` The bootloaders
on the bench units got no answer to their ARP, waited, and booted the stock image they
already had. At 03:40 the controller showed all fourteen Mill Street APs checked in, on
the libraries' build, with their configuration pushed back down.

---

Ines wrote the handover at the storeroom desk before she left.

> **Mill Street, 02:00–03:40.** Switch upgrade rebooted all APs. Returns bench laptop was
> still running `ap51-flash` without `-m` on the AP VLAN and reflashed all 14 ceiling units
> with the stock image as they booted. Reflashed them back with our factory image,
> restricted to their MACs with `-m`. Bench units unaffected (already flashed, then
> excluded by the filter). Laptop is powered off. Lola: your six are done and still
> on the bench; the other six come on today's van.

Two things went into the consortium's wiki that week. The storeroom port at Mill Street
was to move to a VLAN of its own; the change was booked for the following Tuesday. And the
returns procedure got a wrapper, `flash-returns`, which Lola and Ines wrote together on
Thursday. It reads MACs from a file Lola fills in from the label on each unit, passes one
`-m` for each, and exits without running `ap51-flash` if the file is empty.

Lola ran it for the first time on Thursday afternoon, against the six that had come on
Wednesday's van. While they were flashing, someone in the meeting room upstairs unplugged
and replugged the ceiling unit, and the bench laptop printed one more line:

```
[a2:40:1c:07:91:c4]: is of type 'OM2P' but MAC does not match MAC filter
```
